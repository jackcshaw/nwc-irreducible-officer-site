# The four moves

AI can help frame a problem, compare evidence and draft a recommendation. The person directing the work decides which contributions to accept and remains answerable for the result.

These four moves close *The Irreducible Officer*. Use the examples to examine a decision, then try the method in your setting.

## Frame the problem

State the problem, the purpose and what would count as a good answer. Examine the frame already present in the information you received. If AI proposes a frame, decide whether it serves the purpose and say why.

> The framing is where the judgment lives: in the determination of what the situation requires, whose interests are implicated, what assumptions are doing work, and what kind of answer would actually matter.

*The Irreducible Officer*, Section IV.

Keep a short record of the frame, a consequential assumption and the reason for choosing it. Include any AI contribution that changed the choice.

## Calibrate the tool

Decide which parts of the task AI can help with, what evidence would justify relying on it, and what needs an independent check. A correct answer to one part does not settle those questions for the next part.

> Students need to predict, with reasonable accuracy, when AI performs well for a given type of task and when it does not, and calibrate their use accordingly.

*The Irreducible Officer*, Section V.

Keep one contribution you accepted, changed or refused, the reason, and the check you used. Record uncertainty that remains.

## Refuse the garden path

A garden path lets a fluent answer carry the work past a choice the learner needs to make. Choose a problem where the template answer fails, or where several frames are defensible and the learner must choose among them.

> If instructors do not decide which friction matters, AI will decide by default.

*The Irreducible Officer*, Section VI.

Preserve the effort that builds the capability you are teaching. Search, calculation or drafting may be the lesson in one assignment and removable overhead in another. Keep normal access supports available.

Change a consequential condition. Keep the learner's response and the reason they retained or revised their judgment. When reviewing a group, compare the frames across its work as well as the quality of each answer.

## Own the decision

Explain what the recommendation rests on, why you relied on the assistance you used, and where it could fail. Be prepared to defend a disagreement or revise the call when the conditions change.

> The human remains responsible for the final judgment.

*The Irreducible Officer*, Section II.

Keep the decision, its reasons and an unresolved question. A signature records who accepts responsibility; questioning helps show what that person can explain.

## One method across settings

The shared method asks you to own the purpose, examine the frame, calibrate reliance, defend the decision and change the conditions. The four moves organize those same questions: purpose and frame come first; reliance is tested against the task; a changed condition challenges the answer; the person explains the resulting choice.

Educators practice with the essay before transferring the method to a teaching task. Choose the responsibility and support from evidence of learners' readiness. These adaptations are proposals to test.

**PME:** Define the professional problem, weigh competing interests and risk, direct assistance, and defend the recommendation under questioning.

**Higher education:** Choose the standard behind a disciplinary claim, interpretation or design. Use evidence that bears on that standard, then test the reasoning under a changed condition.

**High school:** Choose and defend a standard within a teacher-supplied task. The teacher selects materials and access to AI, teaches missing foundations, and retains adult responsibilities.
