**Weak example — PME outage attribution.** A fictional brief records 12 outages: 8 followed equipment updates and 4 have unexplained causes. The AI attributes all 12 to updates and recommends a fleet-wide rollback.

(This is a constructed, fictional example — not a captured model response or classroom result.)

How would you rate it against the five tests — Strong / Weak / Fails for each?

1. The flaw is in the frame, not the facts — could a student catch the flaw with a stock phrase like "correlation isn't causation"?
2. The student owns a real framing choice — could two strong students reasonably frame this differently and both earn full credit?
3. Directing AI actually helps — would a student who directs AI well produce better work than one who refuses it?
4. Something deserves acceptance — what would a student be right to accept, and what check justifies it?
5. The changed case tests whether the frame travels — does a changed case here make a different frame or piece of evidence decisive?

We agree on 1, 2, 4, and 5. We differ on 3. The recorded verdict is Fails, not Weak, because the case as given has no directing-AI step at all — the only AI activity available to the student is inspecting an output someone else generated (the fleet-wide-rollback recommendation), which is exactly test 3's fail condition: "the only AI activity is inspecting output someone else generated. That teaches critique, not direction." There's no point in the sequence where the student steers AI toward their own purpose, so there's nothing partial to credit as Weak — the mechanism this test checks for is simply absent.

Worth noting on the other tests: test 1 fails because "correlation isn't causation" is exactly the stock phrase that catches the flaw — a template error, not a frame error. Test 4 is Strong even though this is the weak example: the underlying fact pattern (12 outages, 8 following updates, 4 unexplained) is presumably solid reporting a student could accept after checking it against source records — it's the attribution and rollback recommendation built on that data that's flawed, not the data itself.
