# Motion figure conventions

Shared visual vocabulary for the eight figures. The first figures settle these; later figures reuse them. Each entry says which figure introduced it. "FC" is frame capture (figure 1); "FS" is fluency substitution (figure 2); "PS" is premature synthesis (figure 3); "UR" is uncalibrated reliance (figure 4); "ID" is invisible delegation (figure 5); "IM" is institutional monoculture (figure 6); "RL" is responsibility laundering (figure 7).

## Colour and marks

- **Colour grammar** (FC, refining the reel's rule; accepted by the orchestrator and flagged for Jack).
  - Grey (`#5b564f`) is what the AI supplied.
  - Red (`#b81b2b`) is what a person decided or did themselves (their own lines, notes and answers), plus the labels that name that person (all of FC, FS and PS use it this way; CONSISTENCY.md).
  - Navy (`#0a2242`) is the page's ink: the task, labels, and words a person wrote with no decision attached.
  - Paper is `#f6f1e8`; cards and sheets are `#fffdfa`.
  - The reel said "grey and navy are machine work". The figures keep every machine element grey and use navy only as ink, so a change from AI to apparent authorship can be shown as grey turning to ink.
- **AI-supplied text sits on a grey-tinted band** (`#ded5c4`) (FC). A change from grey to ink then shows as a shape disappearing, not only as a hue change between two dark colours.
- **Red appears only for a person's decisions and own work in the picture** (FC). Player chrome, the current-beat marker and the "FACULTY CHECK" label are navy. Red margin marks can read as a teacher's pen, so a red mark should be visibly made by its person (the dot draws it) and named by a red label in the final frame.
- **Dashed means nobody has taken it up; solid means someone did** (FC, from concepts-v0).
- **A frame is corner brackets joined by a hairline** (FC), with a mono tag naming whose frame it is ("AI'S FRAME"). The hairline closes the sides so "inside" is unambiguous. A frame a person chose would be red.
- **The red dot is the person** when there is one person (concepts-v0). Label it in words the first time it appears.
- **Name people the same way in every figure** (CONSISTENCY.md, figures 1–3).
  - Use red Sans 600 13 beside the dot: "Student" while there's one, then "First student" and "Second student". Never "another student" in a label. Captions can keep natural phrasing.
  - In the final frame, name each person once in red, as a column or row head (FS's table heads; FC's labels over the two papers).
  - Ink mono is for things ("FIRST STUDENT'S BRIEF"), not people.
- **Nothing decorative** (FC). Every mark on the stage stands for something named in the storyboard's cast.
- **Constructed examples say so on screen** (FC): "EXAMPLE TASK".
- **Show, don't tell, the contrast** (FC). If a caption describes a second person's process, the picture shows it: a second red dot doing the work, one movement at a time.
- **What a product rests on sits behind it** (FC). A person's reasoning can be drawn as a strip clipped to the area below the paper. It slides up behind the paper to hide and down to reveal, so asking visibly brings it out.
- **Key a colour on screen before it changes** (FC). A small key ("AI-SUPPLIED" with a tinted swatch) points at the tint before the wipe drains it. Show the change centre stage and large.
- **Contrasts are about ownership, not tool use** (FC). A contrasting person who does it right also works with AI: the AI proposes to them too (grey), and their frame turns red only after they examine the alternatives.
- **Origin changes keep the content** (FC). When AI-supplied text becomes indistinguishable, the words stay and only the grey band and colour drain to ink. Blank boxes read as deletion.
- **Drain only when invisibility is the point** (FS). FC drains the AI's grey because its point is that the origin vanishes from the product. When origin isn't hidden, the AI's words stay grey to the end, so nothing reads as hiding authorship.
- **A disclosure tag** (FS). A product made with AI carries a grey mono "DRAFTED WITH AI" tag through the final frame. It shows the AI's part without making disclosure the question.
- **Set aside is a dim of the sheet, not the words** (FS). When faculty ask without the paper, the sheet fades into the page and the grey bands lighten to 55%; the words stay at full strength (5.6:1). An 80% opacity dim of the whole paper drops grey-on-band text to 3.6:1.
- **Set aside can be a move** (PS). When "papers aside" must read as the papers being out of reach, they shrink to 56% and slide out of the people's rows into a strip, slightly tilted. Paths don't cross (the lower paper travels sideways before it rises), and labels fade out and back in rather than sliding across each other. Small text inside the papers fades as they shrink; the titles stay (Serif 19, still 11.5 px at 1,040).
- **Same argument, own words** (PS). Two papers that must be indistinguishable without looking copied share a title, citations and disclosure, differ only in line lengths, and carry a label saying "SAME ARGUMENT, OWN WORDS". Don't let either paper carry what the better student learned (a qualifier line was tried and removed after PS's figure read 1): the caption says "same argument", and the oral test is the point.
- **A source lights up when someone repeats it** (FS), but only where repeating it is the failure, and only after the difference is audible. The band behind the repeated words returns to full strength and a navy underline runs under them, then both fade.
- **A speech panel is what a person says without the paper** (FS). A rounded paper-bright box with the words in quotation marks. Both people's answers arrive at the same speaking pace.
- **Show what faculty hear before showing origin** (FS). Spoken answers arrive in plain ink, because colours are invisible in the room. Origin colours (grey band for the AI's words, red for a person's own) come back only after the difference faculty can hear has landed, as part of the turn.
- **What faculty observe stays ink for everyone** (PS). Sketches and spoken answers are ink for both people to the end, and the difference is in what they say. Process colour (grey AI lines, red own lines) lives only on the students' side.
- **A connection is a line between two cards** (PS). Dashed grey: the AI proposed it and nobody has taken it up. Solid grey: a person took it up as given. Red: a person drew it, or checked it against the sources before keeping it. Lines run centre to centre under the cards, so only the gaps show.
- **A card opens when someone reads it closely** (PS). It grows and shows two ink note bars, and closes when that reader leaves. Two people who read equally closely get the same motion at the same pace, so reading depth can't be mistaken for the difference.
- **A connection that doesn't hold** (PS) gets a red ✕ at its middle and retracts. The ✕ stays at half strength while that map is on screen, so a later absence (the AI drew nothing there) can be pointed at: the ✕ brightens once.
- **What a person learns is a red italic note with no card** (PS). It appears where they learned it and returns, labelled "WHAT THEY LEARNED", when they use it. No card, so it reads as memory and not as notes brought to an oral check.
- **Faculty pointing** (PS) is a navy ring drawn around one line, which thickens while the others dim to 40%.
- **Faculty pointing at a section of text** (UR) is a navy rounded outline around that part in each product. It can move from part to part, so faculty visibly ask about each part in turn instead of going straight to the one that went wrong.
- **A "READY" tag** (UR, shared with figure 8). A filled grey pill on an AI answer means the tool presented it as finished. Right and wrong answers carry identical tags at the same moment, so "equal confidence" is something on screen, not only in the caption. AI answers state their numbers flatly (no "About").
- **Working marks come off at hand-in** (UR). What a person did with each part ("✓ CHECKED", "USED AS IS") is written by their dot as red mono 12 tags on their own product while they work. Before faculty look, the marks lift 6 units and fade under "FACULTY SEE ONLY THE BRIEFS", so the faculty question isn't answered by the paper.
- **Giving the AI material** (UR). Small grey-banded copies slide from each source into a stack beside the AI's column and stay to the end, so later frames still show what the AI was given.
- **A source opens to a heading and its lines** (UR) in a paper-bright callout under its card, and stays open. Put the status in the heading and set the exception apart (UR: "OPEN TODAY / Docks 1, 3, 4", then "✕ Dock 2: closed" in muted italic), never a tag after a sentence, which can read as the opposite ("closed for repairs. OPEN TODAY").
- **Read before the change** (UR, from figure cold read 2). When a correction or decision turns on a source, set what the person read large on their own sheet and hold it about 1.5 s before anything changes, so the reason is read before the result.
- **Define a term in its own beat before using it** (UR, from figure cold read 1). A caption that defines the term, the mark drawn and labelled, and a hold long enough to read (UR: 9.3 s uncovered) before any evidence that uses the word.
- **The jagged frontier** (UR, labels shared with figure 8). An irregular navy line with uneven teeth, drawn only between the look-alike items it separates (never a level divider across the stage), labelled "INSIDE THE FRONTIER" and "OUTSIDE THE FRONTIER". It appears only after the people have worked, and its reason is on screen first as navy pattern marks (UR: "CAPACITY" under the AI's figure, "OPEN TODAY" on the source that has the current number). "Outside" is kept for the frontier; the caption says "on the page" for how things look.
- **An evidence panel** (UR). A study's numbers rise centre stage on a paper-bright panel while the scene dims to 25%, then the scene returns. Equal-height ink bars, values exactly as in the source spine, groups the spine doesn't distinguish kept in one labelled row ("WITH AI · TWO GROUPS"), a mono source line, and a bracket for a finding without a number ("Graders rated higher in quality").
- **A decision ledger in three lanes** (ID). Hairline dividers separate "THE STUDENT DECIDES" (red mono 12), "REQUESTS" (ink mono 12) and "THE AI DECIDES" (grey mono 12).
  - A decision is a card named by what is decided: a dashed ink outline when nobody has decided, grey on a grey band when the AI decided, a red outline and red label when a person decided. A short bar under the label is drawn as the decision is made.
  - Position says who decides, and the AI's lane holds only decisions the AI made. Requests keep their own lane, so a person's words never sit under "THE AI DECIDES".
  - An empty slot is a faint outline with a centred "—", never a dashed outline. The first cold read took a faint dashed slot for the card itself.
- **A request carries a decision in full view** (ID). A request is a speech bubble in the person's words, grown from their dot, that settles as a Serif italic quote line in the requests lane. A request that hands a decision over clips onto the card, and the two travel together across the lanes in one slow movement (2.4 s the first time, 1.4 s after). A card hidden under a bubble read as a disappearing label.
- **What a request left unsaid** (ID) sits on its own line under the quote, outside the quotation marks: a mono 15 "UNSAID" tag, then grey italic words (Serif 15) in a dashed ghost box (no fill). A key, a dashed swatch and "LEFT UNSAID", sits beside the lane heading. Words appended to the quote read as typed.
- **Words in a request that state a decision already made** (ID) are red, with a short red pointer back to the card they refer to. The person writes the card first, then asks, and the caption says that order.
- **An AI's notes** (ID) are a grey-band card of short readable lines (Serif 15). A person ticks each note they act on (ink ✓, about 0.6 s a note) and strikes one they turn down (red line), with their reason in red italic underneath. It shows a standard changing what was accepted while the handed-in products still look alike. The person who turns one down gets their own captioned beat for it.
- **A course standard on the task card** (ID). An ink "RUBRIC ·" line on the task card from the first beat, so both people visibly have the same standard. A person who draws on it gets a captioned beat: their dot travels to it, and the line grows to 130% on a navy highlight and holds at least 1.5 s, then returns to size with a 40% underline.
- **A draft counter** (ID). Ink mono "DRAFT 2", "DRAFT 3", "DRAFT 4" under a small paper shows revision where rewriting inside the paper would be too small to read.
- **The viewer's diagram dims when faculty ask** (ID; extends UR's working marks). Ledgers and working notes drop to 15% while faculty ask, and the products, counters and disclosure tags stay at full strength. The diagram returns under the check as its answer.
- **Evidence size** (ID, from a cold read). The words a viewer must read to see the difference (notes, ticks, a struck note and its reason, "UNSAID" lines) are 15 units, so they render at 13.75 px or more on any drawn stage (the stage switches to the stacked view below a 0.917 scale). A wide stacked stage sets them at 14–15 px.
- **Name the speaker on every speech panel** (ID). Each panel starts with the person's name in red Sans 600 13, so an answer placed beside the AI's lane can't read as the AI speaking. Each dot's home sits beside its name label, so the dot reads as the person from the first beat.
- **A caption that names an order matches the order on screen** (ID, from a cold read). "Writes down each decision first, then asks" read as all three before any request; "Before each request, the second student writes the decision down" matches write-then-ask, three times.
- **A seminar grid** (IM). When the unit is a class, ten small papers sit in a five-by-two grid, each with its student's red dot at the top-left corner, under one red name label for the group ("The seminar"). No student is shown alone or compared with a classmate.
- **Alike first, the words at the reveal** (IM, from figure cold read 1). When AI output comes back alike to many people, draw the copies in one identical grey pattern as they land, one movement at a time, and hold the shared words for the beat that traces the origin. There they grow out of the AI panel as a grey card (IM: "EVERY AI OUTLINE OPENED WITH" over "Counter the rival's influence"), and a grey line draws from the card to what it explains. Shown as the copies landed, the words spoiled the origin beat and were half-read in the busiest second. The card stays to the final frame, so the AI panel never stands empty.
- **Whose frame is a pill** (IM, from figure cold read 1). A grey-band pill ("AI'S FRAME") sits apart beside a frame bracket, where the trace line lands, and the goal keeps its own label. "COUNTER THE RIVAL  AI'S FRAME" in one mono line read as "counter the rival AI's frame".
- **Faculty read each paper** (IM). A navy ring steps across the papers and leaves a small navy ✓ inside each paper's top-right corner. The ticks stay to the final frame: a tick that fades read as approval withdrawn (IM storyboard cold read 1).
- **A context card** (IM). Under the task, a hairline over a short list that fades in at reading pace. Give every item the same kind of owner, or none ("our trade, its ports, its election, its minerals, a rival's loans"), so no item looks planted by the task.
- **A lineup board** (IM, revised after figure cold read 2). Faculty line up what they receive: copies of each paper's one-line problem statement lift out, grow into ink words and settle in a list, while the papers stay where they are. Faculty then read each line (a small navy reading mark appears beside it in turn) and bracket the goal the lines share, named in a mono label ("ONE FRAME: COUNTER THE RIVAL"). The lines share a goal, not a word: IM's ten are worded differently and only three contain "rival". An underline under a repeated word taught a keyword test, and frames sorted by possessive read as a word game. A frame later shown to be the AI's turns grey, with an "AI'S FRAME" pill.
- **A frame still to find** (IM, revised after figure cold read 2) is mono "FACULTY ASK" over a dashed, empty bracket with a centred Serif italic "?". It gets its own beat ("Faculty ask what other frames the task could take.") before any imagined answer, sits in open space aligned with nothing that could answer it, stays empty to the end, and pulses once under the check. Labelling missing frames from a comparison the faculty member doesn't have read as an answer key, and a question that first appeared beside an imagined spread read as answered by it.
- **An imagined run** (IM). A thought experiment (the same people under one change) sits in a block under the real run with the same grid, its name label saying "imagined" and its board headed "IMAGINED: ONE POSSIBLE SPREAD" (a heading that can't be read as the first group's label; "ONE POSSIBLE RESULT" over "COUNTER THE RIVAL" read as one phrase). Its results are readable words, lifted the same way as the real run's, so the contrast is shown and not asserted. The real run shrinks into the upper half and dims to 35% while the imagined run plays, and returns for the comparison. Use it when no source measures the outcome, and say so on the page.
- **The AI's challenge looks like a challenge** (IM, from figure cold read 2). When a contrast run uses the AI to challenge a person's own work, the person's line comes first and the AI's reply is a grey speech bubble of questions, once in words (IM: "THE AI ASKS: What did you assume? What else could it be?") and then small on each product. A smaller copy of the earlier outline sheet read as the AI supplying content again.
- **Same prompt, same draft** (RL, from storyboard cold read 1). When two people must hand in the same AI output, a "Suppose" caption says they gave an AI the same prompt, and one large draft divides into two identical copies that slide into their columns. Identical papers with no reason given read as copying.
- **Nothing red or first-person on or under a handed-in product** (RL, from storyboard cold reads 1 and 2). A red line from a person to a phrase read as a stronger tie to that phrase. Red underlines read as a teacher's corrections. A red first-person statement under a paper read as part of the submission. A person's own working sits in a separate row above the products, labelled "THEIR OWN NOTE · NOT HANDED IN", with a faint "—" for anyone who has none and a hairline between it and the products. It stays to the end (extends FS's "not handed in" row).
- **A note that carries evidence gets a hold and a place** (RL, from storyboard cold read 2). When a note is what shows a later answer wasn't made up afterwards, it holds for its reading time plus 1.5 s before the person moves on, and stays in its row. A note that faded as the person signed read as a concern dropped.
- **A reason handed to the AI** (RL; v0's "Who signs"; reworked after figure cold reads 1 and 2). After an answer that gives the AI's reason, navy outlines first mark the AI's phrase in the answer and on the paper ("The analysis"). Then the person's dot halves (radius 5 to 3.5): one half stays on the signature line, and the other travels to the phrase and turns into a hollow dashed grey ring (radius 7, 16 px at desktop; 12 px on the stacked stage), keyed "REASON HANDED TO THE AI". It stays to the final frame, and the weight passes it and rests on the half dot. A person who gives their own reason keeps a whole dot. A red dot by the AI's phrase read as "decided here"; a small one that rejoined read as "a dot moved" and left the still without the difference.
- **What faculty see** (RL, from figure cold read 1). While faculty ask, a navy frame labelled "WHAT FACULTY SEE" goes round the products, and any row of a person's own working dims to 20% under "FACULTY DON'T SEE THIS". Both go when the viewer's view returns, and the working returns to full strength once it explains an answer. A note left at full strength while the caption said "faculty see only the papers" read as faculty seeing it.
- **A red bracket beside a spoken answer** (RL) marks the answer that accounts for a decision. It sits inside the speech panel, down the left of that answer; the words stay ink.
- **The weight** (RL). A navy block shaped like a weight, labelled "ANSWERABLE", hangs at the top of a paper's margin and falls to rest on the person's dot at the signature line, the dot holding it up. "ANSWERABLE" is the essay's word (Section VII); "ANSWERS FOR IT" read as a noun among spoken answers. It rests on everyone who signs, so it marks who answers for a recommendation, not who failed. It always arrives under a "Suppose" caption, and two weights fall one after the other so each can be watched. On the stacked stage a small navy weight falls down each card's left edge, clear of the text, and becomes the chip at the signature row; a chip that hung or fell over the text covered the phrase that mattered. A dashed outline on the AI's phrase, tried for "can't answer for it", read as "this claim is questionable" and was dropped.
- **A signature** (RL) is a red squiggle the dot draws along a hairline at the foot of a paper. The dot then rests at the line's left end.
- **Reading drawn as reading** (RL, from storyboard cold read 1). A reading dot moves along under each line at 1.0 s a line, with a 0.3 s pause between sentences, on lines set 28 units apart so the dot fits between them. At 0.6 s a line it looked like skimming.

## Camera

- **The camera is how close we stand** (FC). A push-in narrows what can be seen; a pull-back restores it.
- **The caption must say whose view it is** (FC). "Up close, it's all they see" makes the close view the student's; "Step back with us" makes the wide view ours. Without that, a pull-back reads as the person gaining distance.
- **A pan changes which side we look at** (FC). The world can be two panels wide, the person's side and the faculty side. The view pans across while following the thing that travels (FC: the paper). Elements that belong to the viewer, such as the task card, stay put on screen during the pan.
- Moves are eased (`inOutStrong`), at most 2.5 s, one at a time, and removed under reduced motion.

## Type and size

- Source Serif 4 for captions and titles, and Source Sans 3 for labels in the picture: 15 units for things such as considerations and cards, and red Sans 600 13 for person labels. IBM Plex Mono is for small labels.
- **Picture text is 13 units or more** (UR; FC and others raised to it after CONSISTENCY.md). Mono labels are 13. Scaled copies count at their drawn size (FC: strip chips at 87% of 15; paper headings at 16.3 local inside an 80% paper). At 13 units, a drawn stage keeps every label at 1280 × 720 (scale 0.911, 11.8 px). Entries below that mention 12-unit mono predate this rule.
- **Stage:** an SVG with a 960 × 540 viewBox. On the standalone page the figure is up to 1,040 px wide, so 13-unit mono renders at about 14 px at 1440.
- **Narrow stacked stage** (FS; PS reuses it). When the words are the figure's content, below 480 px the SVG can give way to a stacked HTML text view of the current beat, driven by the same `render(t)`, with text at 13–16 px. Collapse the SVG (`height: 0; overflow: hidden`) rather than `display: none`, so its text can still be measured. Each beat shows the sections it is about (a `SHOW` list per beat), and spoken answers appear word by word on the same clock. PS's stage carries the reading names, both maps as link chips (dashed grey, solid grey, red, struck), both papers, each student's order, both answers and the "What they learned" note. Keep the comparison's anchor in view throughout (FS: a "Both handed in this paper" tile), put people side by side as on desktop, and make the final frame, check and controls fit an 844 px screen.
- **Stacked whenever a label would vanish** (UR; the rule for every figure, per CONSISTENCY.md). Check at 1440 × 900, 1280 × 720 and 390 × 844. A drawn stage should never lose labels to `legibility()`: either keep all text at 13 units or more, or switch to a stacked stage when it would. Every figure now does: FC's text is 13 units (legible at 1280 × 720), and its stacked stage takes over below that scale, on phones and in short windows.
  - *How UR does it:* UR switches to the stacked stage whenever its smallest picture text would render under 11 px at the runtime's letterboxed scale (`min` of the width and height fits; a ResizeObserver on the SVG toggles `.is-stacked`), not only below 480 px, so no drawn stage loses its labels. UR sets its picture text at 13 units or more, so the SVG survives the viewport fit at 1280 × 720 (scale 0.91) and anything wider than about 812 px.
- **A stacked stage with a fixed layout** (UR, from figure cold read 1). Every section is placed from the first frame and only fades in (opacity and visibility, never `display`), things are added and never swapped out, the setup and the decisive source stay to the end, and a study or other aside is an absolutely positioned overlay over the dimmed stage. With the caption band at a fixed height (UR: 136 px, 166 px below 720 px), the controls never move; measure it by seeking through the whole run and recording the controls' top.
- **A narrow stage that keeps its height** (ID). The stacked stage measures its own tallest moment (rendering every half second once, whenever the figure's width changes) and holds that as its minimum height, so the caption band and controls never move during play. ID's narrow stage puts the two people in side-by-side columns, each decision as a cell (request, unsaid line, and a chip naming who decides: "Student's to decide", "AI decides", "Student decides"). With the check and controls it measures 828 px at 390 px wide; at 1280 × 720 (where a drawn stage would fall under the scale) it measures 718 px.
- **A narrow stage that grows only when the picture does** (IM). A section that arrives later (IM: the imagined run, the AI panel's opening line) stays `display: none` until it arrives, so the held height is the tallest real moment, not the sum of hidden sections; hidden-but-present sections had pushed IM's stage 140 px over. IM's narrow stage gives each run one row of ten paper glyphs (24 × 31 px) over its board; the glyph that is changing gets a navy outline for that moment so the waves can be followed, and the AI chip pulses when a request or reply travels. With the nameplate strip, the 220 px check band and the controls it measures 838 px at 390 × 844: the AI chip hides once its last message is gone, and each imagined goal is one flowing line.
- **A narrow comparison in two columns** (RL, on UR's fixed layout). Grid columns are `minmax(0, 1fr)` with `min-width: 0` on their children, or a long chip pushes the second column off the stage. A mark that comes and goes inside a line (RL: the half dot before a sentence) is absolutely positioned in the card's padding, so it doesn't reflow the text. RL's narrow stage (names, the note row, both copies with signature rows and "Answerable" chips, both questions, both answers) measures 838 px with the check and controls at 390 px wide, the same in every beat. Overlays that come and go there ("What faculty see", "Faculty don't see this") are absolutely positioned with a paper backing, so they never change the height.
- **Keep the picture on laptops** (IM, from figure cold read 1). When the picture is the evidence (IM: ten visibly different papers), switch to the stacked stage only below 480 px of figure width, and make laptop windows fit instead: at 720 px wide or more and 860 px high or less, a compact caption band (112 px; check and its second line at 22 px, one line each) and a 196 px `--mf-chrome` (about 19 px of slack, so autoplay's "caption and controls fully in view" is reachable with the picture's top at the window's top) let the picture scale to fit, with picture labels at mono 13 so they stay at 11 px or more (12.6 px at 1280 × 720, 11.1 px at 1366 × 657, counting scaled groups). Taller windows keep the full band (176 px for a two-line second line; `--mf-chrome` 268 px). Between 480 px and laptop widths the legibility rule hides small labels and the beat list shows each beat's "On screen" line. UR's rule (stacked whenever a label would vanish) had sent every laptop under about 770 px high to 14 px paper icons. With the nameplate (beat 0, plate strip, name and definition above the check), IM's laptop band is 92 px (check, second line and definition one line each, at 20 px and 15 px) and its chrome 166 px plus the 31 px `--mf-plate`, which keeps 13-unit labels at 11.1 px at 1366 × 657 and 12.6 px at 1280 × 720.
- **Legibility rule** (FC). Picture text carries `data-fs` (its size in viewBox units). `MotionFigures.legibility()` hides any text that would render under 11 px and adds `.is-narrow` to the figure. The beat list then shows each beat's "On screen" line, and the caption band carries the words at full size. Shapes and motion stay.
- **Real words when the words are the point** (FS). FC draws writing as bars. When the failure lives in the wording (fluency), set the real sentences: Serif 20 while they're read, no smaller than 12.4 units once settled, with fixed line breaks if two copies must match line for line.

## Pacing and captions

- **Beat rhythm** (FC, from PLAN.md):
  - The caption changes first, with a 0.3 s fade.
  - Nothing in the picture moves for the first 1.0 s.
  - Movements run one at a time.
  - A change the caption is about starts only after the caption can be read once (words ÷ 3 s).
  - At least 1.5 s of hold follows the last movement.
  - No beat is shorter than twice its caption's reading time.
  - A beat with no movement is allowed when the caption is the event (FC beat 9).
- **Length.** Figures run 30 to about 90 s. FC is 86.2 s in 11 beats; FS is 88.6 s in 10 beats; PS is 90.0 s in 10 beats; UR is 90.6 s in 10 beats, after merging slow beats on the tester's frame-difference drag analysis. ID is 101.6 s in 10 beats: the second person's work takes three captioned beats (the rubric, write-then-ask, the notes), and the key evidence holds long enough to read. IM is 96.3 s in 11 beats, over for the faculty's own question beat and readable imagined lines; every beat meets (caption + new picture words) ÷ 3 + 1.5 s, reached by cutting words rather than adding time. RL is 93.2 s in 9 beats (96.2 s in the player with the name card), with two faculty questions in two beats and a frame showing what faculty see. Every beat lasts at least (caption plus words to read) ÷ 3 + 1.5 s, reached by cutting words first; answers type at about 5 words a second and each stays readable at 3 a second.
- **Reading budget** (UR, from cold reads). A beat must hold its caption plus the new words in the picture at 3 words a second or less, not only its caption. A setup can be split (task, then sources) to stay inside it.
- **Speech pace** (PS). Spoken words appear at about 3 words a second and hold at least 1.5 s after the last word. Both people speak at the same pace.
- **Labels arrive with their beat** (FC). A label that says what something is appears by the time that thing settles, never late in the beat.
- **The setup beat names the situation before anything goes wrong.** The turn beat makes the mechanism visible. The figure ends on the faculty check.
- **Caption band** (FC). Captions are real text directly under the picture, inside the figure's shot area (`[data-mf-shot]`), so stills, video and the poster include them. A mono counter ("3 / 11") leads each caption. On desktop the band has a fixed minimum height (136 px) sized for the check, so the controls never jump.
- **The faculty check is the last caption** (FC). It is styled as the check: a mono "FACULTY CHECK" label over the question in large italic, verbatim from the site's Core Failure Modes table. The picture doesn't repeat it. When the check alone can't separate the cases the figure shows, the separating question sits directly under it at the same size (FC: "Why this frame? What would change it?"). It appears after the check can be read once (`check.follow` with no `followLabel`).
- **Captions name who does what.** No "X, not Y" openings, slogans or morals. Run the copy through `writing-as-jack`.

## Final frame, text equivalent, page

- **The final frame is the reduced-motion view, the print view and the poster.** The poster is the shot area (stage plus caption band). Design it first.
- **Every figure names its failure mode** (FC; standard for all eight, at Jack's request: "I tend to forget which named failure mode we're interrogating"). Pass the runtime's `nameplate` option, below. The figure then:
  - opens on its name, large and centred, with its kicker ("Failure mode 1 of 7"; appropriate reliance uses "The skill"). The card holds 2 s, then moves and shrinks into the top-left corner over 1 s, with the caption band quiet;
  - keeps "NAME · 1 OF 7" in a reserved strip above the stage's top-left corner in every beat and view (drawn stage, stacked stage, reduced motion, print, stills, video), so it never covers the picture;
  - ends with the name and its one-line, role-neutral definition (canonical in `NAMEPLATE-DEFINITIONS.md`, approved by Jack) directly above the faculty check.

  The waiting view before play shows the card over the poster. The beat list gains beat 0, the name card.
- **Say what a person did, not only what they marked** (FS). A struck AI sentence alone reads as the person's writing; a red label ("Cut one sentence from the AI's draft:") says it was a cut.
- **A comparison can end as a table** (FS). Rows are steps (their own work, what faculty receive, each question); columns are people, each headed by their red dot and name. Each item appears large first, then settles into its cell, so the final frame reads top to bottom on its own.
- **Order as a sequence label** (PS). When the difference between two people is the order they worked in, each row carries a compact mono sequence ("READ → AI CONNECTS → WRITES", "READ → TRIES → AI CHECKS → WRITES"), AI steps in grey and the person's decisive step in red, so the final frame carries the cause as well as the test.
- **Hide empty boxes on narrow screens** (PS). A speech panel or note hides with its words when the legibility rule hides them, so phones show no empty boxes.
- **Text equivalent** (FC). The beat list is the captions, each with an "On screen" line describing the picture. The lines are always in the accessibility tree, and visible under reduced motion, in print and in narrow views. The SVG and caption band are `aria-hidden`; a visually hidden paragraph summarises the whole figure.
- **Standalone page** (FC), in this order:
  1. a kicker ("Failure mode 1 of 7");
  2. the title;
  3. a one-paragraph setup;
  4. the figure: shot area, controls (Play/Pause, Restart, scrubber, time) and the beat list;
  5. the faculty check, with the essay's follow-up;
  6. the essay passage;
  7. links to the book chapter, Practice (`judgmentlab.net/#companion`) and the essay section (`judgmentlab.net/#<section-slug>`).

## Runtime (`runtime/motion.js`, `runtime/figures.css`)

Created for FC from `concepts-v0/motion.js` and `figures.css`, and backward compatible with them: a v0 figure (toggle button plus beat grid) mounts unchanged.

- **A figure is `render(t)`,** a pure function of time. Captions and their fades are drawn from `t` too, so seeking gives identical frames for export.
- **`MotionFigures.mount(root, {duration, render, beats, poster?})`.** Each beat is `{at, text, list?, detail?, check?}`. `check` is `{label, question, followLabel?, follow?, followAt?}`.
- **`nameplate: {name, kicker, definition, plate?, card?}`** (FC; every figure passes it).
  - `name`: "Frame capture".
  - `kicker`: "Failure mode 1 of 7", or "The skill" for appropriate reliance.
  - `definition`: the figure's line from `NAMEPLATE-DEFINITIONS.md` (role-neutral; FC: "The first plausible frame becomes the working frame, unexamined.").
  - `plate`: the corner text after the name. The default is the kicker without "Failure mode ", so "1 of 7".
  - `card`: the card's length in seconds. The default is 3: a 2 s hold, then a 1 s move.

  How it works:
  - **Time.** The runtime offsets the figure's clock by the card. `render(t)`, beats, `poster`, `gates`, `lockedAt`, `reducedAt` and `api.seek` stay in figure time, so a figure's own constants don't change. `?t=`, the scrubber, the time readout and `window.__motionFigures[id]` (`duration`, `beats`, `settle`, `seek`, plus `card`) are in player time, which is figure time plus the card. `api.time()` returns figure time; a stacked stage uses it to re-render after a resize.
  - **Layout.** The strip is 23 px. The last caption sets labels in a left column and words in the right (name beside the definition, "FACULTY CHECK" beside the question), so the desktop band grows only to 144 px. `--mf-plate` (31 px) joins `--mf-chrome` in the viewport fit. A drawn stage whose text is 13 units still renders it at 11.1 px at 1280 × 720.
  - **Phones.** Below 560 px the columns stack. Measure the check band at 390 px and fix the caption band at that height (FC: 222 px). Check that the whole player still fits 844 px (FC: 780 px). The card's name is one line (nowrap) at 34 px or more, so a long name overflows a phone's stage: "Responsibility laundering" is 378 px wide in a 356 px shot at 390. RL scales it on phones with `clamp(22px, calc((100vw - 70px) / 11.3), 34px)` under 560 px (28 px at 390).
  - **Compatibility.** Without `nameplate`, nothing changes: FS, PS, UR, ID, IM, RL and AR rendered identically before and after the change (same frames at 1440 × 900 and 390 × 844).
- **Optional hooks inside the root:**
  - `[data-mf-toggle]` (Play/Pause/Replay);
  - `[data-mf-replay]` (Restart);
  - `[data-mf-scrub]` (range input; keyboard accessible, with `aria-valuetext`);
  - `[data-mf-time]`;
  - `[data-mf-caption]`;
  - `[data-mf-beats].is-list` (vertical list with "On screen" details).
- **Helpers added:**
  - `bracketPath(rect, arm)`;
  - `pathAt(segs, start)` for a mark's piecewise path;
  - `legibility(root, svg, viewWidth, minPx)`.
- **Behaviour:**
  - **Controls never move on phones** (PS; every figure, per CONSISTENCY.md). Measure the tallest stage and caption band at 390 × 844 by seeking through the whole run, and fix both: the stacked stage's minimum height, and the caption band's minimum height (160 px, or 166 px when the check needs it). Set the check at 20 px below 480 px. FC measures its stacked stage's tallest beat whenever the width changes and holds it (511 px at 390 px wide), with the caption band at 166 px below 720 px; its player measures 796 px at 390 × 844.
  - **The player fits the viewport** (PS, shared; applies to every figure with a shot). `figures.css` caps the stage SVG at `100svh − --mf-chrome` (228 px on desktop, 250 px below 720 px wide: what the caption band and controls need), so the stage, caption band and controls fit together when first in view. The SVG letterboxes inside its box, and `legibility()` measures the letterboxed scale (`min` of the width and height fits). A figure with its own narrow stage should give it a fixed height (its tallest beat) and fix the caption band's height on phones, so the controls never move.
  - **Autoplay waits for the whole player** (PS, shared). The figure plays once when half its shot (`[data-mf-shot]`) is in view and its caption band and controls are fully in view. Before PS it was 60% of the whole figure, which a long beat list kept from ever happening on phones or at 1280×720. A v0 figure without a shot still keys on its root at 60%. `data-mf-autoplay="off"` disables autoplay.
  - **Pause when scrolled away** (IM, shared, from figure cold read 2). A playing figure pauses once less than a quarter of its shot (`[data-mf-shot]`, or the root for a v0 figure) is in view, and shows Play. It doesn't resume by itself, so a reader who scrolls to the beat list or the page comes back to where they left. Gates, seeks and reduced motion are unaffected; `data-mf-offscreen="play"` on the root opts out. Checked on all eight figures: each mounts, autoplays, pauses away, stays paused on return and ends, and appropriate reliance still stops at its gate and resumes.
  - **Poster** (PS; standard for every figure, per CONSISTENCY.md). Use the settled setup beat, with the task and the people shown before the AI acts: `poster: BEATS[n].at - 0.02` (FC: `BEATS[2].at - 0.02`, the task, the student and six considerations). The runtime option itself stays optional. `mount(root, {…, poster: seconds})` shows that settled moment while the figure waits to play, so the first view isn't an empty stage; play still starts from 0. PS uses beat 1 settled.
  - **Setup copy** (PS). Keep the paragraph above the figure short (about four sentences), and don't tell readers to "pause it" before the controls are on screen.
  - On the scrubber, arrow keys move 1 s and Shift+arrow 5 s (PS; the 0.05 s step stays for dragging). Home, End, PageUp and PageDown keep the browser's behaviour.
  - Reduced motion (`prefers-reduced-motion` or `?reduced`) shows the final frame, and beat clicks jump between settled stills.
  - `?t=12.5` holds a moment; `?t=1:04.5` also works (minutes and seconds; IM).
  - Printing draws the final frame.
  - `window.__motionFigures[id]` exposes `seek`, `settle(i)`, `beats` and `duration` for scripts.

## Scripts (`scripts/`)

Serve `motion-figures/` with `python3 -m http.server 5291 --bind 127.0.0.1`. Don't use ports 5180, 5187 or 5199.

- `node scripts/figure-shots.mjs <slug> [port]` does three things:
  - writes a still per beat (at its settled moment) and the final frame to `figures/<slug>/shots/`, plus reduced-motion and 390/768 px pages;
  - checks overflow, reduced motion, keyboard access and the smallest rendered text;
  - reports console errors.
  - also reports `minRenderedTextPx` at 1440 × 1000, 1366 × 657 and 1280 × 720: the smallest visible picture text at any beat's settled moment, from each text's font size times its screen scale (`getScreenCTM`), so text inside a scaled group counts at its drawn size (IM; `data-fs` alone missed IM's scaled card label).
- `node scripts/figure-export.mjs <slug> [port] [fps]` renders the shot area frame by frame through `seek` into `shots/<slug>.mp4`, `.webm` and `.gif`. It uses the showreel's Remotion ffmpeg read-only.
- Both take `--edition=<id>` (editions pilot, FC). It loads `?edition=<id>`. Stills go to `shots/<id>/` and previews to `shots/<slug>-<id>.mp4/.webm/.gif`. Without the flag, both behave as before.
- `node scripts/frame-diff.mjs <slug> capture <dir> [--edition=id] [--hide=css] [--port=5291]` is the regression check (editions pilot, FC). It captures the shot area at these moments:
  - the waiting view;
  - every beat start;
  - every beat's settled moment;
  - every 2 s and the end;

  at 1440 × 900, 1280 × 720 and 390 × 844. It also captures reduced motion (the figure at 1440 and 390, and the full page at both widths) and the print view. FC's set is 203 PNGs.
- `node scripts/frame-diff.mjs <slug> diff <baseline> <current> […]` captures into `<current>`, then compares every baseline file.
  - Identical bytes count as identical. Otherwise it compares pixels in the browser and reports the count of differing pixels per file.
  - It prints `{captured, errors, compared, identical, differing, missing, diffs}` and exits 1 on any difference.
  - Rendering is deterministic, so a re-capture of an unchanged figure is identical byte for byte (FC: 203/203).
  - Anything that changes layout above the figure moves it. Even a move of whole pixels shifts Chrome's raster tiles and gives single-row differences. `--hide=".fig-editions,.fig-edition-note"` sets `display:none` on intended page additions, so a capture still compares with a baseline taken before them.
  - Keep regression folders under `figures/<slug>/regression/` (PNGs are gitignored).
  - Run captures one at a time, on a server of your own (`--port`, e.g. 5293). A parallel load produced noise in earlier checks. On the shared 5291 server, two captures of the same ID code differed in 128 frames; on a quiet port, the same code matched HEAD in all 203.
  - The print shot waits 600 ms after `beforeprint`, past the beat list's 200 ms colour transition. At 100 ms, shots of the same code differed by one colour level from run to run.
  - Some figures differ slightly even between two captures of the same code (AR: one pixel at 1440 × 900, t = 20). Before calling a difference real, run a self-check: diff the baseline against a second capture of the same code.
  - To check a shared runtime change against other figures, export HEAD to a scratch folder (`git archive HEAD design/motion-figures | tar -x -C <scratch>`). Serve it on a second port, capture each figure there with `--port`, then diff against the working tree on 5291.

## Editions (pilot: frame capture)

One figure, several scenarios: PME (the essay's own examples), business (study-grounded, workplace cast) and, later, classroom. Plan and Jack's decisions: EDITIONS-PLAN.md. Scenarios: BUSINESS-EXAMPLES.md.

**Three layers.**
- **Core** stays in the figure's JS and never changes between editions:
  - the timeline (beat times, motion moments);
  - layout and slots;
  - the colour grammar;
  - the nameplate (name, kicker and the role-neutral definition are universal).
- **Scenario:** `figures/<slug>/editions/<id>.json` holds every visible word: picture labels, stacked-view text, captions, the beat list, the check and follow-up, and page copy.
- **Cast:** `casts.json` (shared) holds role words and check headings.
  - `classroom`: "First student", "Faculty check", "What faculty receive", …
  - `workplace`: "First analyst", "Reviewer's check", "What the manager receives", …

Every string in an edition may use `{key}`, or `{key|upper}` for mono labels. Both are filled from its cast. Add a key to every cast at once, never in a figure.

**Edition schema** (FC's, as the pattern; figure-specific keys are fine if they're semantic):
- `id`, `cast`;
- `task {label, lines[], text}`;
- `considerations[]`: in the core's slot order;
- `narrowing {split?, title: [{add | keep, join?}]}`. This is the AI's restatement as segments. A `keep` segment claims the first travelling word of the task with the same text. An `add` segment fades in. `join` means no space before it. `split` breaks a task word into pieces (PME: "adversary's" → "adversary" + "'s"). PME's `[{add: "Deter the"}, {keep: "adversary"}]` draws exactly what the hard-coded version did;
- `labels {…}`: SVG words;
- `narrow {…}`: stacked-view words;
- `beats[] {text, list?, detail}`: times stay in the core (`BEAT_AT`);
- `check {label, question, follow}`;
- `page {kicker, title, description, setup, srTitle, srSummary, checkHeading, checkLines[], checkFollow, evidenceHeading?, evidence[]?, quoteHeading, quote, cite, linksHeading, links[{lead, text, href}]}`.

**Optional scenario features are gated on data,** so other editions draw exactly as before. Business adds `labels.changeNote {label, lines[]}` and `narrow.changeNote` (the second person's "what would change it"), and `labels.source` / `narrow.source` (the one-line source under the final frame). The core draws a feature only when its key exists.

**Loader** (runtime, additive):
- `MotionFigures.loadEdition(root, {base = 'editions/', casts = '../../casts.json', fallback = 'pme'})` returns a promise of the filled edition plus `{id, castId, cast}`.
  - Edition: `?edition=`, then `data-edition` on the root, then `fallback`. An unknown id falls back.
  - Cast: `?cast=`, then the edition's `cast`, then `classroom`.
  - It sets `data-edition-id` and `data-cast` on the root.
- A figure builds after it loads: `Promise.all([loadEdition(root), document.fonts.ready]).then(([ed]) => { fillPage(document, ed); build(ed); })`. `window.__motionFigures[id]` appears after that, and the scripts already wait for it.
- `MotionFigures.fillPage(document, ed)` fills page copy from these attributes; the page HTML keeps only structure:
  - `data-ed="path"`: text;
  - `data-ed-rich="path"`: text where `*words*` becomes `<em>`, everything else escaped;
  - `data-ed-lines="path"` (+ `data-ed-class`): one `<p>` per item;
  - `data-ed-links="path"`: `<li><span>lead</span> <a>`;
  - `data-ed-content="path"`: a meta description, with the PME text kept as the static fallback;
  - `data-ed-section="path"`: hidden when empty, as for PME's evidence;
  - `data-edition-link="id"`: switch links, with href set and `aria-current` on the current edition.
- **Default:** PME with the classroom cast, so existing links render exactly as before. Jack chose business with the workplace cast as the default for standalone pages and articles; the site sets it at integration with `data-edition="business"` or the link.
- Figures that don't call the loader are unchanged. The seven other figures were frame-diffed against the HEAD runtime.

**Edition switch:** this is page furniture, the same in every edition, so it stays in the HTML under the setup:
- `<nav class="fig-editions">See this with: <a data-edition-link="business">Workplace</a> · <a data-edition-link="pme">PME</a></nav>`;
- then `<p class="fig-edition-note">PME: example from <em>The Irreducible Officer</em>, the National War College essay.</p>`, which tells workplace readers what PME is.

The current edition's link is bold and underlined (`aria-current`). Classroom joins as a third link later. Both lines use whole-pixel line heights (21 and 20 px), so the figure moves by whole pixels, and the figure sits at the same place in every edition.

**Evidence, option A** (business):
- "Illustration based on [study]" in the setup.
- `labels.source` as one line under the final frame (FC: fades in 1 s after the check).
- The full finding on the page in `.fig-ed-evidence` paragraphs: quotes word for word with page numbers, the finding stated in the paper's words, and what kind of system the study examined.
- A stacked phone view too tight for the source shows it under the controls instead (`[data-fc-source]`, outside the fixed-height player).
- Shared classes for editions are prefixed `fig-ed-`. IM already has its own `.fig-evidence`, and a shared rule with that name would collide.

## Interactive figures (appropriate reliance, figure 8)

Added by figure 8 ("AR"), the one interactive figure. Everything here is optional; figures without these fields behave exactly as before.

- **Gates** (runtime, AR). `mount(root, {…, gates: [{at, resumeAt}], onGate, reducedAt})`. Playback stops once at each `at`; `resume()` jumps to `resumeAt` (only a hold remains, so nothing visibly skips) and plays. A gate re-arms whenever the figure is drawn before it (scrub back, Restart). Play at an open gate resumes. `render(t, {gate})` receives the open gate's index (-1 when none), `onGate(i)` fires when playback stops, and `reducedAt` is what reduced motion shows instead of the final frame. `mount()` now returns `{play, pause, seek, redraw, resume, openGate, gateOpen}`, and `window.__motionFigures[id]` also exposes `resume`, `openGate` and `gates`. Verified: figures 1–7 still play and land on their final frame under reduced motion.
- **A pure function of time and calls** (AR). The reader's calls are state that `render` reads; captions never branch. Only the reader's red marks, one pill label and the live-region text depend on them. `?calls=g1:a,g2:move` preloads calls for stills; `scripts/figure-branches.mjs <slug> [port]` exports per-branch stills from `figures/<slug>/branches.json`, plus each question with its controls open and a 390 px final still.
- **Controls over the picture** (AR). At a gate, the drawn options become real radio buttons: invisible labels positioned over each drawn pill (or each chip in the stacked stage), with a visible focus ring and arrow-key movement within a group. Continue and "Skip this question" sit at the bottom right of the caption band, in a wrapper outside `[data-mf-caption]` (which the runtime rewrites each beat). If focus was on the player when a gate opens, it moves to the radios; a polite live region announces each question and each suggested call.
- **Question before answer** (AR). Every view that doesn't play shows the question first: the poster and first frame are the first question; reduced motion opens on it and steps question by question; the beat list and the hidden summary are released gate by gate ("Show all beats" opens them); page copy that gives the answer sits in a disclosure, "After you make your call", which opens when the figure reaches its check; print gives the question, a page break, then the final frame and the opened disclosure, and restores the screen afterwards.
- **A budgeted call** (AR). When checking is the choice, the reader gets a fixed amount of it ("TIME FOR ONE CHECK"), so a call has a cost. What the reader didn't check is labelled as reliance in red ("You rely on it").
- **Suggested calls** (AR). Filled navy tags under a "SUGGESTED CALL" key, the same for every reader, labelled as reasoning from the evidence rather than findings. After each reveal, the options the reader didn't choose fade to 40%, so options and verdicts don't depend on fill alone. Cost notes in ink mono say what each choice costs.
- **The reader in red** (AR). Red Sans 600 13 "Your call" names the open question's row; the chosen option gets a red dot (r 4.5) in its radio and a thicker outline; the final frame's key is a red dot (r 5) with red mono "YOUR CALLS", shown only when the reader made a call.
- **A changed condition adds a row** (AR), so the final frame shows both conditions and both calls side by side.
- **"Start over"** replaces "Restart" on an interactive figure and clears the calls.
- **A long check** (AR). AR's check is longer than the family's, so it sets `.mf-check-q` at `clamp(20px, 2.1vw, 23px)` (one line at desktop widths, keeping the 136 px band) and fixes the phone band at 182 px (measured at 390 px).
- **No passing an unanswered question** (runtime, AR, after figure cold read 1). `lockedAt()` returns the time of the first gate the reader hasn't answered; no seek, scrub, End key or beat click passes it (the playhead stops and the gate opens), and `play()` does nothing while a gate is open. `onResume(i)` lets the figure record the answer or the skip before playback moves on; `onGate(i, via)` says whether the gate opened in play, at a lock or from the figure. A skip is recorded and shown ("No call made"). Locked beat-list entries read "Make your call to see this." and their buttons are disabled; there is no "Show all".
- **Open on the question** (AR). An interactive figure can open with its first gate live (the poster is the question, and its options work), instead of autoplaying; Start over plays the setup.
- **Focus at a question** (AR). When a gate opens in play, scroll so the whole shot shows if it fits (otherwise the options and Continue), and focus the first option; Enter on a chosen option continues; after Continue or Skip, focus moves to Play/Pause. Put the option inputs before Continue in the DOM.
- **A stacked stage that grows at a question** (AR). Parts marked `data-ph` join only after a gate, so the phone stage is compact while the reader decides and changes height only when they continue.
- **Autoplay once, before any play** (runtime, AR). The autoplay observer now plays only if nobody has started the figure yet. Before, a reader who pressed Play and then paused could have the figure restart when the player first scrolled fully into view. All eight figures checked.
- **Play at a question asks; only Skip skips** (runtime `onGatePlay`, AR, after figure cold read 2). Play at an open gate calls the figure's `onGatePlay(i)` when defined (default: resume). AR shows "Make your call first, or skip this question." and focuses the first option, or continues if a call is made.
- **`?t=` respects locks; the window seek hook doesn't** (runtime, AR). A `?t=` hold past an unanswered gate stops there. `window.__motionFigures[id].seek(t)` is the export path and ignores `lockedAt`, so the stills and video scripts still render every frame.
- **A first view that fits the question** (AR). Until the first question is answered, AR caps its SVG so the stage, the question and Continue fit below a compact page top on load (SVG at 1280 × 900, the compact stacked stage at 1280 × 720), without scrolling. After Continue the cap lifts and the picture is brought into view.
- **Figure reasoning is labelled** (AR). A line that is the figure's inference rather than a study finding sits under a mono "WHAT THE EVIDENCE SUGGESTS" label, in Serif italic; study facts stay in mono caps.
- **No answer text in the DOM before its reveal** (AR, after final verification). SVG text that reveals a call is registered with `lazy(node, text, at)` and filled by `render(t)` only from its moment (empty before, so `?t=0`/Start over clears it). Opacity 0 isn't enough: find-in-page, `innerText` and a printed page's text layer still see it. Text in `display:none` or `[hidden]` containers (locked beat entries, gated summaries) is not rendered and is fine.
- **Phones follow the action** (AR). On a stacked stage taller than the screen (≤ 719 px), the caption band and controls are `position: sticky` at the bottom, and while the figure plays the page scrolls gently so the part each caption is about sits above them (a list of `[time, element]` moments); a question's options are brought above the sticky caption when it opens.
- **A nameplate over a live opening** (AR, with the nameplate). A figure that opens on a live moment instead of playing from the start shows the name card over that moment: `api.introCard()` sets the card large over the frame on screen, with the caption band quiet (and `data-mf-card="intro"` on the root, so the figure can quiet its own controls), and `api.settleCard(hold, move)` moves it into the corner on the wall clock. Any draw (play, seek, scrub, a gate, a redraw) ends the intro with the plate in place; neither call changes `t`. During the intro the runtime renders each frame with `info.card` (the card's progress, 0 large to 1 in its corner), so a figure can hold back what the large card would cover: AR's question-1 options fade in from 0.9. AR opens question 1 first, then starts the intro (1.2 s hold, 0.8 s move) once the shot is in view, and settles it at once on any touch, click, key, wheel or focus in the figure, so the card never delays focus or covers the options. The window seek and `?t=` are in player time (figure time + card); a figure's own print and restore code uses `api.time()` and `api.seek()`, which stay in figure time.
- **`.mf-shot` overflow with a sticky caption** (AR). The nameplate sets `overflow: hidden` on the shot, which makes it a scroll container and stops a sticky caption band inside it from sticking to the screen. A figure with a sticky phone band uses `overflow: clip` there instead.
- **A sticky phone band may grow at the last beat** (AR). With the caption band and controls sticky at the bottom, a taller last caption (name, definition, check) grows upward without moving the controls, so AR fixes the band for beats 1 to 9 (182 px) and lets the last caption take its height (264 px at 390). At desktop widths the band stays fixed for every beat: AR sets its definition at 16 px and both check lines at 20 px so the last caption fits the runtime's 144 px from 1024 px up (156 px from 720 to 1023, with `--mf-plate` raised by the extra 12 px).
