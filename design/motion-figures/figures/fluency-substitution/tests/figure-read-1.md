## 2. The point

A polished AI-drafted paper can't show whether the student made the call: two students can hand in the identical paper, and only questioning them, especially after changing a fact, shows which one owns the reasoning.

## 3. The question a teacher should ask afterwards

"Put the paper away. Suppose one fact changes: the ally doesn't want our troops on its soil. What do you recommend now, and which part of your reasoning made you change, or keep, your answer?"

For the teacher's own reflection: "Which of my current assignments would grade these two students the same, and where in my course do I ever change a fact and listen?"

The picture marks the first student's answer as the AI's sentence recited verbatim. A teacher can't tell whether "explain in plain speech" passed both students or caught the first one. Also say that the first question is necessary but not sufficient. *(confusion; caption doesn't match picture)*
2. **Beat 8: the caption runs 5.5–13 s ahead of the picture.** "One student repeats the paper; the other reworks the call" appears at 64.5 s. The changed fact appears at 70 s and the answers at 75–78 s. That's 5.5 s of frozen picture under a caption about something not on screen, and it gives the reveal away. Bring the change-of-fact row in with the caption, or split the caption: "Change one fact." first, then the outcome once the answers land. *(caption doesn't match picture; pacing)*
3. **At 390 px the stage hides all its text, and the student's work vanishes while the AI's stays visible.** At phone width every SVG text element is `visibility: hidden`. AI text still shows as beige bars, because its highlight rectangles remain. Red student text has no backing, so the second student's note and both of their answers render as **empty white boxes**. On a phone, the student who owns the reasoning appears to have written and said nothing, which inverts the figure's point. The key also shrinks to an unlabelled swatch and dot. The site is desktop-first, but this misleads rather than just looking rough: give red text a visible placeholder (red bars), or keep the text. *(ambiguous visual, mobile)*
4. **The colour code is explained 38 s late.** The beige highlight appears at 8 s and red text at 27 s, but the key arrives at 46.5 s, in the shortest beat (6 s), together with a new row label and the caption. Until beat 4 a first-time viewer has no basis to read beige as "AI-written". Bring a minimal key in with the first beige text in beat 2, or at the latest in beat 4, when red first appears. *(ambiguous visual; too much at once)*
5. **"Word for word" is a contrivance that a skeptical teacher will notice.** An AI working from a different prompt reproduces exactly the first student's edited paper, without the sentence the first student happened to cut. It's labelled a constructed example, but the implausibility invites "that would never happen" instead of engagement. The point survives with "the same call, equally polished" or "near-identical". A related point a sharp reader will raise: the second student's handed-in paper contains reasoning they never wrote ("with clear limits to contain escalation"), and their own reason ("troops show we mean it") isn't in it. The figure counts them as owner anyway. *(factual or plausibility claim; fairness to AI)*
6. **The first student is close to a straw man.** They read closely and cut the right line (beat 3). Then, asked directly about an ally that doesn't want troops, they repeat "the forces should go" word for word. The page intro hedges ("it isn't what happens to every student who agrees with an AI"), but nothing inside the figure does. The top row also reduces their contribution to a deletion. A teacher may read the figure as "students who use AI drafts are parrots". A more believable failure would be a fluent but generic answer that doesn't actually adjust, or one that reaches for the paper's language ("weigh escalation…") without a new call. *(unfair to students)*
7. **Beat 3's "agrees with the rest" is barely shown.** The only cue is the relabel to "Student's paper · drafted with AI", at about 22 s during a garbled crossfade. In beat 4 that label shortens to "Drafted with AI", so the ownership cue disappears. *(caption doesn't match picture, weakly)*

The table structure, the figure's best idea, only becomes legible around beats 6 and 7. *(ambiguous visual)*
9. **Beat 2 is dense:** a 38-word paragraph types out in 3 s, with about 50 words to read in 9 s. That's tolerable only because the paragraph persists. *(slightly too fast)*
