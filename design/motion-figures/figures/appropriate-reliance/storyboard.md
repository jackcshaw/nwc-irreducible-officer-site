## 1. Intent

*The cold-read tester does not see this section.*

**The point.** Appropriate reliance means putting your checks where the task needs them, and being able to say why. Checking costs time, and two tasks can get equally finished AI answers, so the answer can't tell you where to check. The task can: whether its right answer depends on something a fluent answer could miss, and what a miss would cost. Sometimes the right call is to rely without a check, because the task has no hidden facts, the AI does it well, and a miss would be cheap and visible. That frees the check for the task that needs it. When a condition raises what a miss costs, the call changes, and sometimes the right move is to buy more time, at a stated cost. Faculty ask for that account: the evidence for relying, and what would have changed the call.

**What a first-time educator should be able to say afterwards.** "I had time to check one of two tasks from a 2023 study of BCG consultants: segmenting the footwear market by users, or picking a clothing brand from a spreadsheet and insider interviews. Both AI answers looked finished, and both were going into my team's first draft. The segmentation sat inside the jagged frontier: with AI, consultants finished more tasks, faster, at higher quality. The brand pick sat outside it: with AI they were right less often, and graders still rated their work higher. The difference was in the tasks. The segmentation had no hidden facts, so a weak answer shows when you read it. The brand pick's deciding details were in the interviews, and the spreadsheet only looked complete, so a polished answer could be wrong and a read-through wouldn't show it. So the suggested call is to rely on the segmentation and spend the check on the brand pick, working it from the interviews myself before comparing with the AI's answer. Then the segmentation was going straight into the launch plan. Same task, same side of the frontier, but now a miss would get built, and the brand pick still needed its check. Moving the one check would leave the brand pick to a read-through, so the suggested call is to ask for a week to check both. That's what I'd ask a student: what evidence justified relying here, and what would have changed the call? Then I'd change one condition and ask again."

**Faculty check** (verbatim from `PLAN.md` and the site's Core Failure Modes table): *What evidence justified relying on the system here, and what would have changed the call?* The final frame adds, directly under it and at the same size, the move the figure makes: *Change one condition and ask for the call again.*

**Essay passages** (Section V, "Appropriate Reliance as a Teachable Competency"):

> The distinction between trust and reliance matters here. Trust is a subjective disposition, a feeling of confidence that a system will perform well. Reliance is the observable act of accepting its output and acting on it. Appropriate reliance is harder and more discriminating: accepting support when it is warranted, refusing or verifying when it is not, and being able to give an account of the difference ([Raees & Papangelis, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23896)). A student who trusts AI generally has learned almost nothing transferable. One who has developed a disciplined account of when and why to rely, across task types, risk levels, and domains, has developed something real.

> AI improved performance on some tasks and degraded it on others, and the boundary was not obvious in advance. Tasks that looked similar from the outside differed in whether AI helped or hurt, a finding Dell'Acqua et al. describe as the jagged technological frontier ... The educational response is pattern recognition: learning to identify what kind of task is in front of you, where models tend to be strong, where they tend to fail, and what independent checks are required before the output governs the work.

> Users who can only inspect an AI answer are still downstream of the frame. Users who can change the task, revise the context, reset the criteria, and design the checks are exercising the judgment the assignment is meant to build.

**Misreadings to design against.**

1. *AI is bad at judgment work, so don't use it.* The first suggested call is to rely on Task A, with the study's inside-the-frontier gains beside it. Task B's ends "then compare". At question 2 the AI's answer stays in use, with checks.
2. *Check everything to be safe.* There's time for one check, so checking Task A leaves Task B unchecked, and the note says so. Asking for more time is offered only at question 2, with its cost in the option ("Ask for a week…"), and suggested only because a miss now gets built and Task B still needs its check.
3. *A check means reading the AI's answer again.* Beat 3 defines a check on screen: work it yourself, then compare.
4. *Checking the AI's answer would have fixed Task B.* The AI groups had the same materials and still scored 70.6% and 60%. The suggestion is to work Task B from the interviews yourself, the closest a reader can get to the group without AI, labelled "reasoned from the results".
5. *You can tell from the answer.* The answers are drawn identically, with the same "READY" tags, and graders rated the AI users' work higher whether or not it was right.
6. *Moving the check is free.* "Moving it leaves Task B to a read-through", the same kind of read that rated wrong answers higher, and the picture shows the check leaving Task B ("Check moved to Task A").
7. *The frontier is fixed, or about subject matter.* "2023" and "SOME WITH GPT-4" are on screen from the start; the reason given is a kind of task.
8. *The frontier alone decides.* Beat 9: same task, same side of the frontier, and the call changes.
9. *It's a quiz with an answer key.* Nothing reveals a call before it's made: not the picture, the poster, reduced motion, print, the hidden summary, the beat list, the scrubber or the page copy. Suggestions are the same for every reader, come with reasons, and are labelled as reasoning. There is no score.
10. *Skipping is the same as agreeing.* A skipped question is recorded and shown ("No call made").

## 2. Scenario

You have time to check one of two tasks from Dell'Acqua et al.'s 2023 field experiment with BCG consultants. Task A: segment the footwear industry market based on users. Task B: use a spreadsheet and insider interviews to pick the brand a clothing firm should back. An AI gives both answers in the same finished form, and both will go into your team's first draft. A check means working the task yourself, then comparing with the AI's answer. You choose which task gets your one check, and you rely on the AI for the other, or skip the question. Then the frontier is drawn between the tasks, the study's results split them, and the figure shows what set the tasks apart and the suggested call. Then one condition changes: your segmentation goes straight into the launch plan. You choose to rely on Task A, move your check to it, or ask for a week to check both, and the figure shows the suggested call and what moving the check costs. The figure ends on the faculty check.

*Sources.* Task A's wording is the paper's ("Segment the footwear industry market based on users", one of 18 product-development tasks for a fictional footwear company). Task B paraphrases the paper's Appendix A (a CEO choosing one of the company's brands to invest in, with a spreadsheet of financial data and interviews with company insiders). "The deciding details were in the interviews; the spreadsheet only looked complete" follows section 3.2. The study figures are from the source spine's Jagged Frontier entry (Dell'Acqua et al., 2023, HBS working paper 24-013): inside the frontier, consultants with AI finished 12.2% more tasks, 25.1% faster and at more than 40% higher quality; on the task chosen to fall outside it, consultants without AI were right about 84.5% of the time, with GPT-4 alone 70.6%, and with GPT-4 plus a prompt-engineering overview 60%, and graders still rated the AI users' recommendations higher in quality "regardless of the correctness of their answer". The AI's answers are drawn as lines, not text. The conditions are added for the exercise. *The Irreducible Officer*, Section V, defines trust, reliance and appropriate reliance, and describes the jagged frontier and pattern recognition across task types.
