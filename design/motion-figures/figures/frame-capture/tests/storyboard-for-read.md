## 2. Scenario

A war-college student must recommend a response to an adversary's military buildup. The student asks an AI for help, and it proposes a reasonable frame: deter the adversary, working from the adversary's intent and allies' interests. The student takes up that frame and writes and revises inside it, without weighing past buildups, domestic politics, room to negotiate or economic pressure. A second student, also working with AI, gets the same proposed frame, weighs all six, keeps the same two, sets four aside, and writes the same paper. Asked why this frame, the second student can show what they weighed; the first can show only the AI's two.

*Sources.* The task, the six considerations and the AI's title, "Deter the adversary", are constructed for this figure, and the task card says "Example task". *The Irreducible Officer*, Section II, warns that a reasonable frame can "define the problem too narrowly" or "assume a theory of adversary behavior". "Room to negotiate" and "Economic pressure" are other kinds of response, so the narrowing from "a response" to "deter" is visible to a non-specialist. The two students follow Section I's point that two papers "will not tell you by themselves" which author understands the problem. The questions are Section VIII's. The figure shows no numbers.

## 4. Beats

Times are absolute seconds. In every beat the caption changes first (0.3 s fade) and nothing in the picture moves for the first second. A change the caption is about starts only after the caption can be read once.

### Beat 1 · 0.0–6.0 s · The task

- **On screen at the start:** an empty paper stage.
- **What moves:**
  1. 1.0–1.6: the task card fades in and rises 6 units at top right.
  2. 1.6–2.0: the red dot grows in at (80, 270), with its "Student" label.
  3. 2.0–6.0: hold.
- **Caption:** "A student gets an open strategy task."
- **Notice:** the red dot is the student. The task asks for "a response" and doesn't say what kind.
- **Why:** the setup names the situation before anything goes wrong.

### Beat 2 · 6.0–14.0 s · Many things could matter

- **What moves:**
  1. 7.0–9.6: six dashed considerations appear one at a time (0.35 s each, one every 0.45 s), alternating top and bottom rows, left to right: Past buildups, Allies' interests, Adversary's intent, Domestic politics, Room to negotiate, Economic pressure.
  2. 9.6–14.0: hold.
- **Caption:** "Many things could matter. Framing means picking which ones define the problem."
- **Notice:** six equal considerations around an open space. Nobody has picked yet.
- **Why:** the whole problem is in view before the crop, with a plain definition of framing.

### Beat 3 · 14.0–23.0 s · The AI proposes a narrower frame

- **What moves:**
  1. 15.0–15.4: the red dot pulses once. The student asks.
  2. 15.5–16.2: grey brackets spring tight around the open space (x 140–510, y 130–410), with the hairline. The student is just outside.
  3. 16.2–16.5: the tag "AI'S FRAME" fades in.
  4. 17.7–18.5: a grey copy of the task sentence lifts off the card, which stays as it is, and travels to the top of the frame.
  5. 18.5–19.5: the copy narrows. Every word except "adversary" fades, the possessive included. "Adversary" slides to the end of the title line, and "Deter the" fades in in front of it on a grey band.
  6. 19.5–20.8: "Deter the adversary" holds alone.
  7. 20.8–21.5: Adversary's intent and Allies' interests slide into the frame as headings, shrinking to 85% and turning solid grey.
  8. 21.5–23.0: hold.
- **Caption:** "The student asks an AI. It proposes a reasonable, narrower frame."
- **Notice:** the frame is labelled as the AI's first. Then "a response" visibly shrinks to "Deter the adversary", and the frame takes in two of the six.
- **Why:** this is the moment the first plausible frame arrives. The narrowing starts 3.7 s in, after the caption can be read once, and holds before anything else moves.

### Beat 4 · 23.0–31.0 s · The student takes it up

- **What moves:**
  1. 24.0–24.8: the dot moves into the frame, to the start of the first row (162, 224). Its label fades.
  2. 25.0–26.9: the camera pushes in to 1.6× on the frame. Everything outside leaves the view.
  3. 27.1–29.1: the dot writes draft 1, eight ragged rows at 0.25 s each. "STUDENT'S DRAFT 1" fades in.
  4. 29.1–31.0: hold.
- **Caption:** "The student takes up the frame. Up close, it's all they see."
- **Notice:** the student steps in; nothing pushes them. Then the rest of the problem goes out of sight.
- **Why:** this is "once accepted" plus the mechanism of distance.

### Beat 5 · 31.0–38.0 s · Revisions

- **What moves:**
  1. 32.0–33.6: draft 2. The dot runs down each column's margin in turn, drawing a red change bar, and the rows straighten into six.
  2. 34.1–35.0: the dot draws a red bar and a four-bar chart grows under the right column.
  3. 35.3–36.2: the dot draws a red bar and sweeps a thick conclusion across both columns, stopping at its end.
  4. 36.2–38.0: hold.
- **Caption:** "The student revises twice, adding evidence and a firmer conclusion."
- **Notice:** the paper gains a chart and a conclusion. Every red mark is the student's. The grey title and headings never change.
- **Why:** the effort is real, so the failure reads as structural.

### Beat 6 · 38.0–45.3 s · Step back with us (the turn)

- **What moves:**
  1. 39.0–41.4: the camera pulls back to 1×. The four dashed considerations and the task card return, exactly as they were. The dot does not move.
  2. 41.4–45.3: hold.
- **Caption:** "Step back with us: every revision stayed inside the AI's frame."
- **Notice:** we see the whole problem again; the student, still at the end of the conclusion, doesn't. Every red mark sits inside the grey boundary.
- **Why:** this is the mechanism made visible, with the distance made ours.

### Beat 7 · 45.3–55.3 s · What faculty receive

- **On screen at the start:** the student's side at 1×, as beat 6 left it.
- **What moves:**
  1. 46.3–48.5: a clean final copy of the paper lifts off the frame and travels right, while the view pans with it to the faculty side. It stops centre stage at full size (sheet x 250–608, y 150–381).
     - The copy carries the grey title band, the grey headings with their words, and grey brackets around it. It has no red marks.
     - The task card stays where it is on screen.
  2. 48.3–48.7: "WHAT FACULTY RECEIVE: THE FINAL COPY" fades in above it.
  3. 48.8–49.2: the key fades in to the right of the paper: a tinted swatch, a leader line pointing at the title, and "AI-SUPPLIED".
  4. 49.2–50.0: nothing moves while the caption is read.
  5. 50.0–51.4: a wipe crosses the paper from left to right, its front marked by an ink line. Behind the line, the tint is gone and the title and headings keep their words in the same navy as the rest.
  6. 51.6–52.4: the grey brackets and the key fade away.
  7. 52.6–53.6: the paper moves to its place at top left, shrinking to 80%. The centre label fades as it leaves, and "WHAT FACULTY RECEIVE" and "FROM THIS STUDENT" fade in as it settles (53.4–53.8).
  8. 53.8–55.3: hold.
- **Caption:** "Faculty receive the final copy. Nothing in it shows the frame came from the AI."
- **Notice:** the key says what the tint means before it drains. After the wipe, the title and headings read as the student's own, and once the brackets go nothing shows the frame came from anywhere else.
- **Why:** "the frame capture is invisible inside the final product", shown big enough to see and labelled so it can't be mistaken for tidying up.
