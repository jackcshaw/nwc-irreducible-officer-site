## 2. Scenario

A war-college student wants to improve a draft paper recommending whether to keep a forward base. The task card carries the course rubric: evidence must back the call. The student asks an AI for feedback, then a better structure, then a more strategic version. They work through every note and revise after each request, through four drafts. Each request names only the kind of help. Each carries a decision to the AI: the criteria, the argument, what "strategic" means.

A second student, with the same task, starts from the rubric. Before each request they write the decision on its card:
- "Evidence must back the call";
- "Lead with the call, then its evidence";
- "Serves the ally's long-term interest".

Then they ask for the same help, with words that point to the card: "against my criteria", "for my argument", "as I define it". Both get the same AI notes. The second student judges them against those decisions and turns one down: "Be more decisive", struck, with "evidence too thin" written under it.

Both papers come back revised with AI and look alike. Faculty, who have only the papers, ask each student "Better by whose standard?" The first says "The AI's notes, I suppose. I worked through what it flagged." The second says "Mine, set first from the rubric: does the evidence back my call?"

*Sources.* The task, the rubric, the notes, the decisions, the requests, the papers and both answers are constructed for this figure, and the task card says "Example task". *The Irreducible Officer*, Section II, defines invisible delegation and supplies the request pairs. Section V describes structured prompting with explicit purpose and evaluation criteria. The figure shows no numbers.
