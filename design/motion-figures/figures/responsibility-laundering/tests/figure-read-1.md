## 2. The point (my words)

Two people can sign the identical AI-drafted paper and both be fully answerable for it. The paper can't tell you which of them can actually account for relying on the AI; asking "why did you rely on it?" can.

## 3. The question a teacher should ask afterwards

"If both of these papers came to you, what would you ask each student, before the port closes, to find out which of them actually made the call to wait?"

## 4. The final frame alone

It mostly works if you read it. It doesn't work at a glance. The final frame shows two identical signed papers, each with an identical "ANSWERABLE" tag on the signature. One student has a private note above their paper; the other has a dash. Both questions are printed, with four answers, and a red bracket beside the second student's "why" answer. The faculty check is in large italic.

What it says to me: both are on the hook; only one could say what the estimate assumed. That second half lives entirely in the text of the answers plus an unlabelled bracket. The image that showed the first student leaning on the AI (the half dot beside "The analysis estimates") is gone, because the halves rejoin in beat 8. So the final frame shows the evidence, not the laundering. A teacher who glanced at the poster would see "both answerable" clearly. They would have to read two answer boxes to get "only one can account for it."

## 5. Problems, ranked

1. **Caption and picture disagree in beat 5 (and through beat 6).** "Faculty see only the papers," yet the "not handed in" note stays fully visible while the questions are asked, and nothing shows a faculty point of view. A viewer may think faculty can read the note. That weakens the core move: the paper is identical, so only the questioning reveals the difference. Dimming or veiling the note row during the questioning would fix it, and so would anything that shows what faculty actually see.

2. **The "good" student admits the very gap that breaks the plan, and the picture never says why that's still the better answer.** Faculty ask "Suppose the port closes," and the second student says "I didn't check for closures." Some teachers will read the second student as just as negligent and ask why they're the model. Others will take away "a fluent explanation equals good judgment." The text below the figure handles this ("Whether relying on the estimate was justified is a separate check"), but nothing in the figure does. One line in beat 7 or 8 would cover it, for example that answerable isn't the same as right.

3. **"Harbor reports: normal" versus "I didn't check for closures" is confusing.** Aren't harbor reports a check for closures? I worked out that it probably means current reports were normal and the student didn't check whether the port could close during the evacuation. A teacher shouldn't have to work that out. One word would do it ("today's harbor reports", or "didn't check forecasts for closures").

4. **The beat 7 "split" is too small to carry its meaning, and the final frame undoes it.** A 7 px dot (4 px at 390) halving and drifting to "The analysis estimates" reads as "a dot moved" unless you lean on the caption. Nothing links the moved half to the first student's words "The analysis estimated…", which is the actual laundering move. Then beat 8 rejoins the halves, so the final frame loses it.

5. **At 390 the key motion of beat 8 is missing.** The "ANSWERABLE" chips fade in beside the signatures instead of travelling past the AI's sentences, so "The AI can't answer for it" has no picture on phones. Beat 3 is also static there for 7 s.

6. **Slow opening.** Beat 1 is 6 s of a task card and two dots on a mostly empty stage. Beat 3 adds another 7 s. Nothing distinguishes the students until 0:30. A teacher showing this in a session might lose people before the point arrives. Nothing is too fast.

7. **Legends sit far from what they label.** "WRITTEN BY AI" appears at the bottom left, about 300 px below the sheet and three seconds after it. "THEIR OWN NOTE · NOT HANDED IN" sits over the first student's column and appears after the second student's note, and the first student's "—" is easy to miss.

8. **"Language distributes agency" isn't drawn.** The setup paragraph names it, and the draft's "It is recommended that civilians wait" is the perfect agentless example, but the figure never points at it. Only "The analysis estimates" gets attention. This is a missed opportunity, not an error.

9. **Fairness check.** The AI is treated fairly: its estimate is never shown to be wrong, and the figure is about the humans. The first student is treated fairly enough, since they do say "I signed it". "Leans on the AI" rests on their answer 2, which is reasonable. The only fairness worry is problem 2.

10. **Factual claims I'd want sourced (page text, not the figure).** Below the figure, the faculty-check paragraph says "The companion's failure-mode lab makes a related point about a signed statement of responsibility… faculty have to sample rather than question everyone." That deserves a link to the lab file. The figure itself makes no numeric claims.

11. **Minor runtime quirk.** Pressing End and then Home on the scrubber leaves `data-state="ended"` at 0:00, though the button correctly says Play. Viewers won't notice.
