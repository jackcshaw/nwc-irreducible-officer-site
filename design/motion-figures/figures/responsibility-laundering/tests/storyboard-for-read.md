## 2. Scenario

Two war-college students must recommend when to evacuate civilians from a port city. Suppose the same prompt gives both the same AI draft: "The analysis estimates ships can carry everyone out." "It is recommended that civilians wait for ships." Both read it closely and agree. The first signs it. The second first writes a note they won't hand in: the estimate rests on an open port, and today's harbor reports look normal. Then they sign. Faculty receive two identical papers, both drafted with AI. They ask each student, "Suppose the port closes. Who made this call?" The first says, "I signed it. The analysis said ships could do it." The second says, "I did. I chose to rely on the estimate." Then they ask, "Why did you rely on it?" The first says, "It was the AI's estimate. It seemed solid." The second says, "It assumes an open port. Today's reports looked normal, so I accepted it. I should have checked for closures." If the recommendation proves wrong, the AI can't answer for it, and both students will.

*Sources.* The task, the draft, the papers, the note and all four answers are constructed for this figure, and the task card says "Example task". *The Irreducible Officer*, Section II, defines responsibility laundering and names "assumptions no one examined". Section VII says AI "cannot own what follows", describes conviction-shaped output (Andres, 2026), uses "answerable", and asks students to say "why they accepted an output". The figure shows no numbers.

## 4. Beats

Times are the figure's own clock. The player adds the runtime's 3.0 s name card in front (beat 0), so player time is 3.0 s later. In every beat the caption changes first (0.3 s fade) and nothing in the picture moves for the first second. A change the caption is about starts only after the caption can be read once, except a lead-in that the caption's first clause describes. Every beat lasts at least (its caption plus the words the viewer must read in it) ÷ 3 + 1.5 s. Two waits are trimmed at the orchestrator's request (noted in the beats): answers type faster than they're read, and the weights hover 2.5 s.

**From the essay** (*The Irreducible Officer*, Section II, What the Problem Looks Like):

> Responsibility laundering is the final danger. A recommendation becomes easier to defend because the model generated it, or easier to soften because the model's language distributes agency. The analysis lands with the apparent weight of objectivity. But AI does not become accountable for the recommendation. The human remains responsible for the final judgment. When the recommendation proves wrong, shaped by assumptions no one examined and optimizing toward a target no one explicitly chose, the question of who chose the frame does not have a satisfying answer.

And from Section VII, Accountability Is Structural:

> … A human recommendation brings context with it. Experience, incentives, reputation, and the way a person answers when pressed all travel with the recommendation. A commander can weigh those signals. A model carries none of them and can still sound equally confident. It can support a judgment, but it cannot provide the human presence that makes accountability clear to subordinates, partners, or commanders who will live with the result.
>
> First-person ownership matters in the classroom. The student should be able to say why they accepted an output, why they rejected one, and why they remain accountable for the recommendation despite the system's contribution.
